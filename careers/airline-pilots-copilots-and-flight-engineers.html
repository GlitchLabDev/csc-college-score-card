How you get in

Not an entry-level job: it usually takes a degree plus years of related work.

  1. Bachelor's degree
  2. Less than 5 years of experience in a related job
  3. Moderate-term on-the-job training

Common questions

How long does the training take?
Typically about four years for the degree, then a few months to a year of training on the job, then less than 5 years of related work first. That is the usual route rather than a rule — people arrive by other paths all the time.
How much do airline pilots, copilots, and flight engineers make?
The US median is $232,140 a year, meaning half earn more and half less. Pay varies widely by state, employer and experience.
Do you need a degree for this work?
Yes, and employers also expect years of related experience first.
